# Semantic Arts Governance

### CAUTION: This document is out-of-date and not fully aligned with more authoritative sources of truth. Details provided here on the precise duties of the governance committee should not be viewed as authoritative.

# Background

Semantic Arts is an employee-governed company.  Semantic Arts is owned by a trust, but all operational decisions will be made by the president and governance committee on behalf of the employees.  The trust, The Semantic Arts Trust, owns all the stock and therefore intellectual property of Semantic Arts.

This document lays out the expectations that the Semantic Arts Trust has of the president and governance committee.  The Semantic Arts Trust may alter this agreement at any time.

# Stewardship and Servant Leadership

In most companies, leadership is a prize coveted because it often comes with the perk of determining your own pay package.  Leadership is also generally associated with power.

We are promoting a servant leadership model.  In the servant leadership model, the first priority is to promote the growth of the followers.  The servant leadership model is not about assuaging the leaders' need for power, or for them to disproportionately acquire the resources of the firm.

The leadership team, and especially the president and governance committee, will be the final arbiters of bonus distribution, including to themselves.  The expectation is that the leadership team will be fair and transparent.  Disproportionate self-dealing of available bonus amounts will not be tolerated.

That said, servant leadership is not a vow of poverty.  The leadership team should rightfully be compensated, both for taking on the additional responsibility, which is over and above that of the remaining employees, and for their success, both individually and for the results of the organization they are leading.

Every year the president and governance committee’s total remuneration, and the rationalization behind it, will be presented to the employees at large, who will vote anonymously on whether they find the arrangement fair and equitable.  In the event that a majority of the employees find the arrangement inequitable, the governance committee will be disbanded and a new committee established, per the guidelines on selection of the governance committee.

# Mission

The mission of Semantic Arts is to deliver alternate approaches to enterprise information systems implementation that aren’t normally promoted by firms that are focused on short term profit.

We believe that there are significant economic and strategic benefits available to firms that take a measured and disciplined approach to their systems implementation, but this measured and disciplined approach is not consistent with most software and service vendors, who typically need to provide short term financial results.

# Membership in the Governance Committee

As long as the company has fewer than 100 employees, the governance committee will consist of five members.  Above 100 employees it will be expanded to seven.

Members will serve staggered three-year terms.  Two members will come up for re-election in one year, two the following and one the third year.  (This will be three/two/two for a seven-member committee.)  A member must be an employee in good standing and have been with the firm for at least two years.

Members will be elected by anonymous vote of all the full-time employees who have been with the firm at least one year. In a year when two positions are up for re-election, the employees with the most and second most votes will be selected.  On the initial sitting of the committee, the first two vote-getters will get the three-year term, the third and fourth the two-year term, and the fifth the one-year term.

If a member quits the firm or the governance committee, there will be a special election to replace them, and the newly elected will serve out the same term as the person they are replacing.

# Goals of the Governance Committee

The governance committee’s primary goals are to:

- Set long term goals.
- Allocate resources toward those goals.
- Ensure that the voice of the employees are heard.
- Ensure that the President is held accountable.

# Duties of the Governance Committee

The governance committee is set up to complete some of the more collaborative aspects of planning and decision-making.  It is expected that the committee will meet on at least a monthly basis in order to stay abreast of any issues that may take more of their time to discharge.  In times of stability there may not be much need beyond the monthly status check-in, but members should be prepared to intervene when needed.

At least one meeting per quarter shall be without the president, so the governance committee can reflect on the president’s performance in his/her absence.

The duties of the governance committee include:

- Setting the overall company strategy.  The strategy will include which markets and segments to target, how to reach those targets, which technologies to pursue, and setting billing rates and target utilization.
- Setting and monitoring budgets. This includes target revenue and headcount budgets and target discretionary budgets.
- Establishing and executing the employee bonus program. In profitable years we expect a portion of the profit to be retained as a buffer against future shortfalls and a portion to be distributed to the staff.  The bonus should recognize contribution beyond what was already compensated.
- Overseeing the coaching and employee development programs, including the training budget and its use.
- The governance committee will receive a small monthly compensation for these responsibilities.

# How the President is Selected

The president will be selected by the governance committee.  The president may be a member of the governance committee; if so, they will resign from their governance committee duties. The president may even be an outsider, although this should only be considered if the governance committee decides that there are no good candidates available from the employee ranks.

The president will serve a four-year term.  At any time, the governance committee can vote to remove the president.  There are no term limits.

# Goals of the President

The goals of the president are to:

- Ensure the survival of Semantic Arts. The first issue is to make sure the firm persists.  If very bad times hit, the president may need to take draconian measures, including mass layoffs, in order to assure the firm survives and lives to fight another day.
- Maximize the probability of profitability. The goal of the firm and of the president is not to maximize profitability, but to maximize the probability of profitability.  In a privately held, profit-seeking firm, the goal is often to maximize profitability.  This is often seen as good shepherding of resources.  More often it is to maximize return to shareholders or to make the firm more attractive to acquirers.  We do not have these concerns.  Maximizing profit (in the short term) can reduce the firms’ resilience and ability to be profitable in the future.  One way to maximize profit in the short run is to pay employees less.  This is damaging in the long run.
- Achieve the growth goals of the firm. The long-term demand for services that only Semantic Arts provides is huge.  We must grow in order to satisfy that demand.  There are naturally limits to how fast professional services firms can grow, and experience (our own and others) suggests that 30% per year is probably the upper limit to growth, that can still maintain culture and quality. The current growth guardrails are 10-30% (inflation adjusted) per year.  The governance committee may adjust these values over time, especially if we begin to provide subscription revenue to some of our clients.
