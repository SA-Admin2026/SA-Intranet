## Semantic Arts Policy on Positions and Personnel Development

Semantic Arts needs a wide variety of skills to carry out its strategic objectives.  Many of these skills are not available in general population.  Our particular school of design is not yet taught or embraced anywhere.  We have essentially an apprentice model, where we learn on the job from each other.

### Career Development

We recognize that three of the great traits for a fulfilling career are:

·        Purpose / Meaning

·        Autonomy

·        Mastery

At Semantic Arts we attempt to promote all three.  We hope people can find meaning in aligning with our goals for transforming the state of the information system industry and supplying help for specific clients along the way.

Our team-based approach to work and flat organizational structure promotes autonomy with a structure that ensures one can work on mastering their specialty.  As staff grow, they will continue to be afforded additional independence both in the execution of client projects as well as their choices for what to pursue when they are not working on client projects.

This document and career development, in general, are primarily concerned with helping you achieve mastery in your chosen avocation.

One of Semantic Arts core values is “Lifetime Learning” we expect our consultants to continue to find opportunity to learn and apply new knowledge.

### Position Management

We have set up a number of positions that represent levels of skill and experience.  Client Billing Rates will be set based on these positions.  We will perform periodic reviews to ensure our rates remain competitive.  We also occasionally have to audit these rates to support master service agreements.

There is a ladder of positions within each of the three primary skill areas (Ontologist, Developer and Analyst) ranging from Intern and Entry level to Master.  There are general and specific criteria for advancing within a ladder.

We also wish to encourage staff to broaden their abilities and will make provisions for lateral moves between ladders.

## Common Expectations Across Skills

We expect the advancement from entry level through to master to share many attributes across the skill areas. Some of the path similarities include:

·        **Directed to Self-Directed to Leading** – An entry-level person is expected to get specific direction both as to the tasks to undertake and how to go about them.  As a consultant matures, they take on more ability to manage their own activities, initially with assignments similar to their previous ones, but eventually across diverse assignments.  As they become comfortable managing themselves, we eventually expect them to lead others, directly and by example.

·        **Internal to Client to External Focus** – When a consultant joins us, they naturally think primarily of themselves: their career, their immediate concerns.  As they grow and internalize their role, we expect them to gradually shift their focus to become client focused.  With our type of work, a consultant who can see and act on what is best for a client, without being prompted, will be adding value for the firm.  Eventually the consultant who can see and resolve the mutuality of what is best for our clients along with and not in opposition to what is best for the firm, will make the most lasting contribution.

·        **Bloom Taxonomy from Rote Remembering to Creating** – The Bloom Taxonomy outlines five levels at which a topic or domain could be taught and/or learned.  At the lowest level is learning that is merely remembering or recognizing.  This is the level of learning characterized by memorizing information for a test, without necessarily understanding any deeper significance.  One level up, understanding, we begin to deal with the meaning of the domain we’re dealing with.  On up, through to the top level of creativity where we have mastered a domain to the level that we can make original contributions.  We expect our consultants to take each assignment as an opportunity to raise their Bloom-level competence.

·        **Doing to Designing to Architecting** – As someone joins the firm, we will have them “doing” things (building taxonomies or ontologies, writing code, building dashboards or analyzing data).  As they progress, a greater portion of their time should be more design-oriented (even if they are still doing); for example, more about the design of an ontology or the design of a program or a dashboard.  Finally, we expect them to move to a level where their design is meta; that is, they are designing architectures to make doing and designing easier.

·        **Specialized to Versatile** – when someone starts off, we expect they will get good at one technology, tool, or method.  Maybe that is R2RML, maybe that is NLP maybe that is Tableaux, javascript, D3, or TopBraid.  As they advance, we expect them not to rest on the laurels of their first few technologies, but to continue to add more arrows to their quivers.  The more someone advances, the more they should look at a problem space and consider many more solution possibilities.

·        **Short-term to long-term horizon** – Jacque Elliot has promoted a theory that a) people have inherent internal planning horizons, and b) that their planning horizon should correlate with their supervisory level.  Manufacturing line workers typically plan a few days in advance.  Supervisors tend to work at the week to month level.  Management in months, and owners in years or decades.  We expect entry-level people to have relatively short-term planning horizons, but as they advance, they need to be thinking in quarters and years, as we plan major offerings, train people and work through cyclic downturns.

·        **Compoundwords to Spaced Words to Hyphenated-words** – we also expect you to develop a sense of humor.

## Common Expectations per level

### Entry Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

An entry-level employee can contribute to a project with supervision.  When assigned tasks, they may need time to learn the tools and approaches, and we expect entry-level productivity and quality.

### Staff Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

A staff member should be able to deliver quality work products with at least one tool or method that they have specialized in. Often this is in the data mapping or SPARQL area.

### Associate Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

An Associate has a high level of proficiency in at least two tools or methods, and exposure to more.  If they are not proficient with an appropriate tool, they will be aware of it, and know when it should be employed. Able to propose pretty good ontology models that follow SA best practices, though should probably still consult/review with more senior ontologists.

### Journeyperson Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

On many projects the journeyperson may be the most senior person on the project, and as such will be expected to have broadened their point of view to the point where they can coordinate the work of the other disciplines.  We expect the journeyperson to be primarily operating from the client’s point of view. The journeyperson will be designing the solutions they are working on. Able to propose very good ontology models on their own that follow SA best practices.

### Senior Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

A Senior in any of these positions is someone who few question their judgement.  (We need to be clear here, somebody will question even the most competent person, and this is partly a matter of marketing (consider a Master an Expert with marketing)).  What we’re looking for in senior consultants is someone who consistently delivers high-quality products that are highly desired.  A Senior is so far from the merely competent that clients would be upset if we swapped out a Senior (or anyone above that).  They correctly understand that our lower levels are closer to commodities, but as we get to Senior and above, they recognize that the competence is much higher than the billing rate increment.

### Expert Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

An expert is someone who is executing at the top of their field.  Their work products are as good as can be found out there in industry.  There are many “false experts” out there.  Take the guy who did the refinery ontology for Platts.  He is generally held to be an expert, but we wouldn’t consider him one.  Or Deb McGuiness who bills herself as one of the one or two best ontologists in the world (Merck told me this).  Deb is competent, and I’m sure I’d put her in the top 100, but top is a stretch.  We want our experts to be humble experts.  I’d like them to be in the top 50 without bragging about it.  We also expect our experts to be taking a lead in conversations with clients about the future and possibilities.

### Master Ontologist / Developer / Analyst

On the execution level, we expect a master to execute at a level that few can even compete.  Additionally, at this level we expect the Master to be magnanimously (but also strategically) sharing.  While we can’t always avoid training our competitors, we don’t want to focus on this.  We want to be executing work and presenting the results as a byproduct.   A Master should be a magnet for recruiting staff as well as clients.  We are looking for master's similar to Martin Fowler, Neal Ford, Michael Hammer. We don’t know of anyone who has reached this level on the ontology side.  Dean Allemang has a book but not really the followership.  Same with Jim Hendler and Amit Sheth.  I guess we would consider Barry Smith a Master.   Tim Berners-Lee is a Master, but at the architect track more than the ontology track.

## Expectations for Advancement to Each Level

### From Entry Level to Staff

We are looking for demonstration of competence in at least on tool or technology employed at that level.  Absent prior experience with the tool or technology, we would expect most entry-level peoples to progress in 6-18 months.  We are not looking for “check the box” competence.  We are looking for the kind of competence that comes from many months of heads down work, finding the patterns, getting the muscle memory.

### From Staff to Associate

At the staff level we expect people to deliver quality product with little supervision.  Moving to Associate is primarily about versatility, about gaining expertise with a wider variety of tools. We also expect a significant increase in productivity.

### From Associate to Journeyperson

The time in the Associate role should be a time to begin shifting from doing to designing, and also the beginning of developing longer term horizons for execution.  The associate should be taking opportunities to design at higher and higher levels.  We will also be looking for the consultant to learn a client’s business at higher levels in the Bloom taxonomy.  To progress, we expect that after being on a client project for six months, the consultant would understand their scope of the client’s business at the same depth of understanding as an internal subject matter expert of several years’ experience. (It’s not that we expect them to be smarter than our clients, but our approach to knowledge acquisition should lead to more rapid, deeper understanding of the subject matter).

### From Journeyperson to Senior

A lot of this progression is discovering, recognizing, and applying patterns.  Pattern forming and using is at fairly high level in the Bloom taxonomy.  To progress to senior, we would like to see some evidence of running projects and being dropped into demanding situations and thriving.

### From Senior to Expert

Becoming an expert is a matter of mastering a domain at the highest level.  This includes understanding the domain at the top Bloom levels.  It also involves becoming very productive.  We ascribe to Malcolm Gladwell’s theory that becoming an expert typically takes at least 10,000 hours of intentional application.  That is, 10,000 hours of just doing the same thing over and over doesn’t make you an expert.  It requires some coaching and some very intentional application to get better and better.  We have discovered that several years’ experience raises productivity by orders of magnitude.  An expert can see flaws in a design or approach that a senior or journeyperson might take weeks or months to see if they see it at all.  Indeed, this may be one of the deciding principles.  Everyone can criticize and see things they don’t like in a design or approach, but our experts are those that see the profound.  The profound is the revealed truth, that once expressed, everyone sees, but until it is expressed it is invisible.

An experts’ competence should inspire the people around him or her, without their being overt about it. It will take many years to make this progression.

### From Expert to Master

At the expert level, a consultant is rising to the highest level of performance in the field.  To promote to the Master level, we expect an outward shift.  On aspect of the outward shift will be mentoring and teaching our consultants and clients.  Another aspect of the outward shift will be the development of offerings that are perceived to be of value to the market (tools, ontologies, architectures, methodologies, training programs and the like, with the common denominator being that the market recognizes their value).  The final aspect of external reinforcement of mastery is the becoming a thought leader in the industry.  A thought leader may be an author or books or magazine articles, or an in-demand speaker.  Becoming a thought leader is helpful to the firm for recruiting as well as obtaining new clients.
