At Semantic Arts, we want to build a culture where feedback—both positive and constructive—is shared openly and with good intent. That means developing the skills to receive feedback without defensiveness, and to give feedback in a way that’s respectful, direct, and genuinely helpful.

To support that, we’ve put in place some processes for sharing feedback within project teams.

# **Feedback at the End of Each Project**

When a project—meaning a specific contract (SOW or task order), not the engagement as a whole—wraps, two types of feedback should be provided, one within the team and one to the company as a whole.

## 1:1 Team Member Feedback

Everyone on the team will both give and receive feedback to every other team member, including the project leader, in short, virtual, 1:1 meetings.

## Project Debriefs

See the [Project Debriefs](https://semarts.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/CP/pages/3338174465/Project+Debriefs) page.

# **Ongoing Internal Feedback**

For internal developers, we’ll do a broader feedback and debrief every six months.

# **If You’re on the Bench**

For internal projects, we will use the same procedure when the project comes to an end. If you are on projects with no specified end date (such as working on gist), you could always proactively request some feedback from others you work closely with, and present a project update at an OKE or Friday meeting.

# **For Sales, Marketing, and Admin**

For sales, marketing, and admin folks we will plan for 6-month reviews similar to the developers. The structure of this is still TBD, so feel free to weigh in with your thoughts on what might make sense.

# **What to Focus on When Giving Feedback**

Whether you’re giving or receiving feedback, keep it grounded in these three questions:

1. What were your overall impressions?
2. What could the person have done better?
3. What did the person do particularly well?

In general, the first step in dealing with an issue with a colleague should be through an open and direct 1:1 meeting with the colleague in question. Only if the issue is not resolved should you escalate it to HR or leadership.

However, If the feedback is especially sensitive or serious, please talk with HR first. We’ll help you think through the best and most appropriate way to approach the conversation.

# Personal Reflection

Finally, we’d encourage you to build this into how you think about your own work on an ongoing basis. After every project, take a moment to reflect personally:

- What went well?
- What could I have done better?
- What would I do differently next time?

This kind of introspection is also a great thing to bring into coaching conversations. It helps you grow—and helps us all build a culture of honest, respectful feedback and continuous improvement.
