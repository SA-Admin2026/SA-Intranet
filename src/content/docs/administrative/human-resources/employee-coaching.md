**Semantic Arts Coaching Program**

Semantic Arts has made the intentional decision to separate career coaching from supervision/management from promotion. The coaching program is designed to support staff career development through regular check-ins with a senior team member. This document describes some of the goals the coaching program seeks to accomplish and outlines things out of scope.

Much of the idea for the coaching model came from Dave’s own experience working with business coaches. The goal of these relationships is not about getting your next promotion, but rather to have a neutral party to help you meet your professional development goals.

**Primary Goal:** To support and assist members of the team in growing to be well-rounded masters of their craft.

**What to Expect:**

- The ability to speak freely in confidentiality (with some limitations\*) with a coach that is not your direct project manager or supervisor.

- Quarterly coaching sessions, with the option to meet more or less often.

- A loose agenda for each meeting to provide some structure, though you are encouraged to adapt this to your circumstances

- The burden is on you as the coachee to come to the meeting prepared with questions, discussion points, and goals.

**Your Coach Is Someone Who Can…**

- discuss skills you’d like to learn and provide suggestions on how to get there.

- offer another perspective on challenges you are facing.

- help hold you accountable for meeting your professional goals

- offer ideas on where and how to seek out professional development opportunities.

- listen to your suggestions about how Semantic Arts could improve. (These will be conveyed to Dave and/or HR at your coach’s discretion, and anonymously if you prefer.)

**Your Coach Is Not There To…**

- map out your career aspirations and tell you how to get there.

- lobby for a pay raise or promotion (it won’t work anyway).

  - Promotion is an external recognition of internal growth – your coach is there to help with the internal part!
- rant to about serious issues with coworkers, clients or projects. These discussions are better left for HR, the President, or your project manager.

*\*Coaching discussions are generally confidential, except in serious cases where management intervention may be required. Please do not hesitate to speak directly with the President or HR on these matters in accordance with the procedures outlined in the Employee Handbook.*
