# Core Competencies

We want to score potential employees against these capabilities (maybe these are in order of importance?)

**SA Value Alignment:** Create Value, One Voice, Share Equitably, Lifetime Learning, Show Respect, Have Fun

**Consultant Mindset**: Executive presence, curious, communicator

**Modeling Aptitude**: Inclination for the simple and elegant, elicitation skills, adaptive

**Technical Competency:** RDF/S, OWL, SPARQL, ETL Tools (e.g., TARQL), Python, higher-level concepts (Open World Assumption, when to use OWL vs SHACL, value of semantic vs. relational technology, etc.)

## Applicant Positioning

For purposes of this document, we are going to define 4 classes of applicants:

1. Not Qualified: This person is not ready for the role, and we don’t feel they will likely be soon. This could also be a person with the technical capabilities but not aligned with our values or possessing the consultant mindset (not eligible in future), or could be someone with the right characteristics but not technically ready (eligible in future).
2. Entry Level: This person is project ready but will be expected to level up skills quickly. They should have enough SPARQL experience to read and write basic queries, along with a solid foundational understanding of RDF/S and OWL. They must show a basic aptitude and inclination for modeling data in a way that aligns with the Semantic Arts methodology. In terms of soft skills, we should be comfortable with them in a client-facing role, but with no expectation of actually leading a meeting.
3. Intermediate Level: This person possesses solid SPARQL abilities (read and write) and some finer nuances of RDF/S and OWL. They have some experience with other ETL tools and likely some degree of Python experience. Their modeling exercise should have revealed relative ease of reaching the foundational pieces of the model, solid elicitation skills, and an ability to pivot with the introduction of new information. We would be comfortable with this person leading some client discussions, though supervised. We would expect a person at this level to be ready to lead a project in the near future after gaining a better understanding of the Semantic Arts methodology.
4. Advanced Level: This person has many years of direct, hands-on experience with SPARQL and deep understanding of RDF/S and OWL. They have worked in multiple triple store environments and with a variety of ETL tools. The modeling exercise was a breeze, with this person able to capture both the essence of the exercise as well as the finer details very quickly. They likely have experience in a consulting role and are very comfortable communicating with clients. They ideally have experience leading teams and coaching others in semantic technology. They have an in-depth understanding of higher-level concepts such as the value of semantic technology over relational, the use of OWL vs SHACL, etc. It is likely we knew who they were before they even entered our interview process.

## Interview Process

Within each of these calls, there must be room for spontaneity, to deviate from the plan to probe a particular topic deeper. There may also be some overlap between non-technical and technical questions, especially depending on the interviewer.

### Call #1: HR Screening

- Primary goal to be informational, basic resume questions, provide info on process to candidates.
- Set the expectation of where we *think* the candidate falls in the 4 levels above.

### Call #2: Non-Technical Interview

- Interview with an ontologist. Schedule for 30 minutes.
- Conducted by an ontologist with roughly equal or more experience than the candidate and at least 6 months on the job at Semantic Arts.
- Technical abilities is not primary outcome, but pass along any information learned.
- Primary focus is on their **aptitude, lifelong learner, overall presentation, ability to answer questions thoughtfully, etc.**
- Rate candidate on the 1-4 scale based on impressions, identify any areas to focus on for follow up in either technical interview or audition.

### Call #3: Technical Interview

- Scheduled for 45 minutes, notified in advance it is technical and confirm they are ready for that.
- Conducted by an ontologist at associate level or above and at least 6 months on the job at Semantic Arts.
- 5 potential test areas:

  - SPARQL: Focus on exploring a data set and fundamental query writing
  - RDF/S and OWL
  - Python
  - Modeling: Basic modeling exercise to get them thinking this way and probe a bit into their way of thinking
  - SHACL
- Need to standardize to minimize prep time for the interviewer and attempt to have a fairly equal comparison of candidates.

  - Maybe a few more technical ontologists help JT come up with this in 5 modules.
- Identify rating, along with areas to focus on for follow-up in audition.

### Final Interview: Audition

- Candidate is notified to plan 2 hours and to bring a dark pen/paper for some modeling exercises. NDA is signed before the interview.
- Conducted by President, HR, CEO (optional), and 3 ontologists (ontologists can be of any level, but should be a mix of levels to see how the candidate interacts with those more junior and more senior).
- First 45 min dedicated to Q&A (new questions plus any follow-up items mentioned previously).
- Modeling exercise (review rubric and notes beforehand - JT to include link in email with candidate materials).

  - If President and CEO are not present, ask someone must read the script to start the exercise.
- Final 20-30 minutes for candidate to ask us questions. Each person share who your client is and what you are working on.

### Debrief Meeting

- Invite each person that was involved in any phase.
- Schedule as soon as possible after the audition so the result is fresh.
- Primary question: Would you want this person on your project?
- Final assessment on level if decided to make an offer.
- Someone should take notes to reference in the future in case the candidate re-applies.

### Final Decision and Offer

Made by the President.
