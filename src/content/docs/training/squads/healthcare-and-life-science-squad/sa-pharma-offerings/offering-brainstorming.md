There is synergy between clinical trials offering and eCTD offering - regardless of who’s running the clinical trials (a drug development company or a CRO), the drug development company needs to submit the clinical trials results as part of the eCTD for the NDA (New Drug Application), hence our eCTD offering can be specifically extended to package clinical trials reports (Module 5 of eCTD) and become an extension to the clinical trials offering.

The only pending requirement is to come up with 1-2 competency questions for each of the 2 identified use cases. I used chatGPT to brainstorm some questions I would have if I were to decide to proceed with developing a drug:

1. Disease Target Identification

   1. Can we establish **causal relationships** between targets and disease phenotypes?
   2. Do we have internal experts across relevant **therapeutic areas** and **molecular biology**?
   3. Is the target aligned with our **portfolio strategy** or **therapeutic focus areas**?
2. Clinical Trials

   1. Do we have internal or partnered **therapeutic area experts** to guide trial design and endpoints?
   2. Do we understand and comply with **GCP (Good Clinical Practice)** and global regulatory frameworks (e.g., FDA, EMA, ICH)?
   3. Are we equipped to manage **clinical trial applications**, safety reporting, and **IRB/ethics submissions**?
   4. Can we maintain **audit-ready documentation** and **data traceability**?

If I were in the shoes of a person managing the development of a portfolio of drugs, starting from disease target identification, I would want to filter through multiple pass/ fail scenarios as quickly as possible to see where I can reduce likely candidates in subsequent stages using my own proprietary data, in house experts, and whatever pattern recognition algorithms I can throw at the problem.

If I were in charge of clinical trials, I would want to make sure there is sound clinical trial design, data capture, and efficient reporting of results to expedite the decision making capabilities of the relevant governance boards I am filing my New Drug Applications (NDA) for. The cleaner this area is, the tighter the reporting, the faster the review committee should act in approving this for manufacturing and sales. Likewise, for me, doing all this more efficiently allows me to expand the potential portfolio of trials I can run at a time, because there is less room for error if my operational work is being coordinated through a knowledge graph that cuts out the data cleaning and rework.

After careful consideration around how to best fulfill the three deliverables requested of an offering (Problem Statement, Ontology, Competency Questions) in the context of the use case of disease target identification, and having done the analysis of existing competitive offerings, our squad suggests that gistPharma not evolve through the development of a single use case, but rather expand according to the various kinds of prospects' interest encountered.

We propose to go back to the offering and Spark project being called gistPharma, while the focus of our near term efforts will go into developing light models for two use cases:

1. Clinical trials
2. Disease target identification

We realize our core competencies lie in broad knowledge representation and silo integration, as opposed to being subject matter expert that can produce a single scientific offering outside of a client engagement. Our proposal takes into consideration the different use cases on the horizon being sought out by different kinds of organizations such as:

1. PSI CRO:

   1. Clinical trials
2. J&J:

   1. Competition analysis
   2. Integrating lab notebook data
   3. Drug progression decision making
3. Regeneron:

   1. Establishing a semantic data foundation for AI

Ultimately, we view gistPharma as a pharmaceutical enterprise knowledge graph accelerator that has starter kits for multiple use cases. By taking a wider lens approach to developing this offering, we will be able to familiarize ourselves with different use cases and support pre-sales engagements with credible knowledge of the work involved.

### Brainstorming thoughts

IF: Our goal is to create a marketable competitive offering that will resonate with an executive. My thinking is that our audience are (vice) presidents of Pharmaceutical R&D and their immediate teams. Some of the decisions among other they must make are:

- which diseases to pursue drug development for
- which molecular modalities to invest in

Drug target identification plays a part in this decision making and there are already multiple public solutions that I don’t think are beneficial for us to reinvent. I think where we can uniquely shine is at a higher level, potentially harmonizing trusted public solutions under one roof with the ability to plug in companies' internal data. Another strategy I hear a lot about that could be served by the same offering is existing drug repurposing for rare diseases.

I envision an offering that will support Pharma executives in making a range of business decisions, something that supports **portfolio strategy** in pharmaceutical R&D.

IF+AI:

Pharmaceutical R&D Presidents/VPs **identify therapeutic areas** by considering:

1. Unmet Medical Needs: Diseases with poor or no treatments, high prevalence/severity, and significant patient impact.
2. Market Opportunity: Large patient populations, growth potential, favorable reimbursement, and a competitive landscape where they can offer unique value.
3. Scientific Feasibility: Understanding disease biology, recent scientific advancements, available technologies, and the company's internal expertise.

**Drug target identification** is a core component of scientific feasibility. It involves understanding disease biology to find druggable targets, assessing the availability of research tools, and evaluating the scientific risks. Without viable targets, pursuing a therapeutic area is scientifically less feasible.

**Drug repurposing** fits into the process of identifying therapeutic areas as follows:

1. Alternative Focus: Repurposing a known drug for a new disease can become the primary focus if there's strong evidence it could work. This is attractive due to faster development timelines and reduced risk since the drug's safety is often already understood.
2. Strategy Within an Area: Even after selecting a therapeutic area based on unmet needs and scientific feasibility, repurposing existing drugs can be a key strategy to find treatments more rapidly or to gain insights into the disease.

**Drug modality selection** is tightly linked to choosing therapeutic areas and targets. The disease and target often suggest the best modality (e.g., small molecule, biologic, gene therapy). A company's expertise and infrastructure in a specific modality can also drive their therapeutic focus. Each modality has unique risks and development paths that influence decisions. Market competition and cost also play a role.

Ultimately, the **choice of therapeutic area, target, and drug modality** is an interconnected process driven by scientific opportunity, strategic fit, and commercial viability.

IF: I think our offering would be better positioned if it was about selection of therapeutic areas and everything that goes into it.

PW, KC, IF - Dimensions:

Problem statement-ish: the business need is to be competitive in the field, for that executives must wisely chose where to put research efforts, this reflects in the portfolio strategy they develop, the earliest component of which is **selecting therapeutic area focus** based on unmet needs, scientific capability, market size, and competitive landscape.
