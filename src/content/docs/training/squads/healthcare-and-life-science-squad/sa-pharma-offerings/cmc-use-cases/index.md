3.2.S.2.5 and 3.2.P.3.5

#### Aseptic Process and Sterilization Validation

Aseptic process used when something can't be sterilized.  
Sterilization required use of high heat or radiation, and if drug ingredients can't handle this, aseptic processes have to be used,  
e.g. use a clean room or a clean box with gloves inserted from the side.  
Sterilization, for example, uses autoclave that uses high heat and steam.  
E-beam radiation is also used to sterilize in e-beam tunnels and isolator chambers.  
Sterilization is used to sterilize equipment and consumables (e.g. vials, filling lines) used for drug development process.  
Aseptic processing involves maintaining a sterile environment throughout the manufacturing process to prevent contamination. This includes sterilizing all components (e.g., drug products, containers, closures) separately before they are combined in a controlled environment.  
<https://www.crbgroup.com/insights/pharmaceuticals/aseptic-processing-vs-terminal-sterilization>

Validation:

- for the aseptic process - test how well is the environment monitored. Media (broth) rather than the drug product to fill vials and syringes, because it is more susceptible to contamination. Later vials and syringes are examined for contamination of the media. 0-tolerance is an acceptance criteria, expecting 0 growth.
- for sterilization process equipment - sensors and indicators are introduced, e.g. in the autoclave heat sensors are used to determine that the steam is hot enough evenly throughout the entire Biological indicators, like a spore strip or pore line, coated with bacteria are placed the autoclave, run the sterilization, then incubate to see if anything grows. 0-tolerance is an acceptance criteria, expecting 0 growth. Sensors for radiation are called dosimeters, then measure if the radiation is high enough, they are put in multiple positions of the e-beam tunnels to evaluate if the radiation is high enough throughout the entire tunnel.

#### Filter validation

Filters are used to filter out microbiological contamination from drug material in the process of its development.

Validation: testing if filter pore size increases when exposed to the drug to the degree that it lets the contaminants through. Soaking filter in the drug material in a controlled environment, e.g. temperature, for a duration of time. Another test is to pass microbiology through the filter. Bubble point test soak filter in the liquid (water or drug material), pass nitrogen (?) gas through the filter, measure at what pressure it starts bubbling, and compare to the acceptance criterion for this pressure.

#### Transportation qualification

Test different modes of transportation (sea, air, ground) and shipping container types by doing drop, shock (vibration), and heat tests. Shipping container types - passive or active (powered, e.g. refrigerated train car) CTSS - controlled temperature storage system.

### Stability Data

3.2.S.7.3 and 3.2.P.8.3

Drug is placed in a controlled environment (e.g. some temperature) and a number of key aspects are measured at defined intervals to see if they change over time. E.g. main peak measures the stability of the active ingredient, acidic/basic peak measures impurities or degradation products that are acidic/basic in nature.
