[Recording](https://datacentric.sharepoint.com/:v:/s/staff/EaZuQ7CHMxZBmkr6Fdz9UzgBEoH6YpxAcMEj75k283xXNg?e=9hIfPA)

Dylan: Lots of naturally occurring things have informational content, like the rings on a tree trunk. Could be useful to expand gist:Content to cover things like this.

Rebecca: Would not want to extend gist:Content in order not to make it too vacuous.

Michael: DNA sequencing - essentially just a big pile of data. Would probably make a subClass of gist:Content - something called :geneSequencing or something.

Jaime: Upper ontologies are used to having large broad classes. We represent almost everything as text so that we can read it.

Dave: Not so much that we risk being vacuous it’s that everything could be considered gist:Content. One class and then done. We should be actually modelling it and representing it. Votes for not extending it to cover gene sequencing. But when we are trying to understand it then yes. Life Science Subgist

Mark: Content vs formatted content. Agrees with Dave. When you want to go deeper in understanding then extension makes sense.

Heather: Oral history? Biased towards anything written down is covered. Does gist:Content cover things that aren’t written down? Dave: it does have to be digitized to be content. Mark: Content or formatted content?

Michael: Software vs DNA sequencing. Do we have a concept for ‘work’ (ie Moby Dick). Used to have something but Dave thinks a work is just IP in gist right now.

Rebecca: Vacuous - meant one class and everything else is under it. The thing vs the recording of thing. Software written down is one thing but it being executed is not software. Address is content, an address is content but it points to a location.

Mark: Agree with IP and content. Trying to find the distinction. Content is disembodied version of something. Is and ID IP? Once you write it down does that make it ContentExpression?

Scott: Content has to be digitized? (Dave may retract it). Nothing indicating that in gist. Content is a subclass of Artifact that is human made. Two ways to think about content. 1) it is carried by a certain signal, changes of probability 2) broader form. DNA is more about what can be represented - most of it is junk code that doesn’t mean anything.

Jamie: IP that’s not content: Mickey Mouse is IP but is not content. A movie with Mickey Mouse in it, until it is digitized, not sure if it’s content yet. A film that is not digitized is an artifact not yet content

Peter: Phenotype is content expression or not?

Rebecca: Slippery slope on the meaning of the word ‘expression’. Once a patent of trademark expires it is no longer IP but it is still content.

Mark: Even though he’d say Black hair and blue eyes are an expression, but it’s not a content or not a content expression (also not man made)

Dave: Content vs IP, think of work before you think about expression. Have to express it first and then the work is derived from them. Have to create a patent first, then any expression is an infringement.

Rebecca: for a certain time period.

Mark: Mickey Mouse movie is a story which is an expression and content in any way that we put it down.

Michael: If it’s property someone owns it, if it’s copyright then nobody owns it. It was IP before your patent, so why wouldn’t it stop being IP when the patent expires. Rebecca: in a legal sense?

Peter in conclusion: Should continue this conversation.
