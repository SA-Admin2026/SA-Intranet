One role of HR is to provide an open door for employees to come with questions, concerns and complaints. It is essential you provide a non-biased, safe place for a person to share. While no two situations are exactly alike, here are a few guidelines.

* Write out information in as much detail as possible. Ask as many questions as you can think to ask, then repeat information back to the person to ensure you understand
* Try to determine what the goal of the complaint is…are they looking for you to simply hear them out, looking for an employee to be reprimanded, project change, etc.
* Seek to gather information from other parties who may have knowledge of the situation, but don’t reveal information about the complainant. There are potential legal problems if you do not work to conceal the identity of a complainant as much as possible. You should work to gather facts from others in an open-ended way

  + “I heard about a potential issue and was wondering if you could share your perspective…”
* Document everything! Write out details of a spoken conversation as quickly after it happens as possible to avoid losing information
* Once you feel you have the whole story, the most likely best step is to take to Dave. If that isn’t appropriate, potential options would be other senior managers, legal counsel, etc.
* Complaints could lead to a performance improvement discussion, could lead to simply filing the complaint with no action, or could lead to immediate termination depending on level of severity.
