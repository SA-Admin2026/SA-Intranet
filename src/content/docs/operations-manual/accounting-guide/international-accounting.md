### General Principles

We are setting up overseas companies in order to pay employees locally.

Generally, we treat overseas employees similar to domestic. So far the only difference is the UK is much more generous with employers contributions and the like, and so the sharing arrangement is only 45% of earned revenue rather than 50%.

The subsidiaries are 100% owned by semantic Arts. They have a local director, which is the first employee in that country.

We don’t intend for there to be much profit in each subsidiary, and the taxes should be quite simple. We have CPAs in each country.

### Logistics

We have a quick books company set up for each subsidiary. They are in local currency, whether that’s what it looks like in QB or not.

See appendix 3 for how I’m setting up Canadian subsidiary as far as Canadian payroll is concerned

### Payroll

Both payrolls are run through Rippling. See the Payroll section of this manual for specific notes on tasks and related things for international pay runs.

### Related Tasks

Outside of payroll, the following tasks need handled for the subsidiary accounts

**Canada**

* Enter the health insurance payment. If the bank balance gets low, make sure to have Dave top off and enter the intercompany income (memorized transaction)
* Enter the bank fees as appropriate
* Balance the checking account roughly monthly to ensure all transactions are covered and the balance isn’t low

**UK**

* Balance the checking account monthly
* Enter any bank charges
* Make sure to report the in-flow from Rippling for Pension, as well as the out-flow from NOW Pensions
* Watch the balance to ensure it doesn’t creep down too low - shouldn’t need much outside of fees and occasional reporting costs.
