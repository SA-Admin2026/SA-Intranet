## General Notes

This document intends to capture all our accounting procedures, conventions and the like, for the following purposes:

* Train internal bookkeepers
* In case we need to explain our finances to others

This document will go from the general to the specific here.

## A Few Key Things For Our Existing Accounting Procedures:

* Use memorized transaction lists and previous entries to help make the correct one going forward
* Do not enter bills or other AP, just print and place them in the AP/AR folder in Dave's office
* Print payment remittances and place them in the AP/AR folder in Dave's office
* If a physical check is received, give it to Dave for endorsement. After deposit, staple receipt to stub and leave in AP/AR folder

## General Accounting Approaches

We are a professional services firm. Almost all of our revenue comes from billing for our time. Many of our projects are fixed price or not to exceed, but we manage and report on hours for them all.

We generally do a small number of large projects in a year, and therefore we are on the "completed contract" method of recognizing revenue, which is based on accruals. We are on the cash basis for taxes. Our external CPA, derives the cash from the accrual, for reasons that have to do with QuickBooks, but also with the nature of our accounting conventions we can't just push a button and get a cash report. We obviously have our cash balances and aged receivables. We can age our AP, as we enter them all as payables, but we generally just pay soon after receiving them. Occasionally for subcontractors we will enter their time and accrue the payment until we are paid.

Our payroll is on a four week cycle, and therefore there are usually 13 pay periods in a year. Consultants pay is adjusted based on their chargeability and write offs for projects they are involved in. The exact formula is in another document, but in brief it is based on a moving average of the last 52 weeks of their personal contribution to revenue, adjusted for project write-offs. The same factors go into the calculation of employee pay as to the earning of revenue which tends to insure that both calculations are grounded. Our pay structure helps keep costs in line with revenue, for as chargeability goes down, so do average wages. Conversely there is automatic bonuses when chargeability goes up.

We often employ subcontractors. If we are managing them directly we have them enter time into our time control system and accrue the earned revenue just as we would our own.

We also accrue non hourly subcontractors and travel related expenses, which typically make up 10-15% of our projects. Some projects we bill and are reimbursed for specific travel expenses. For others we create an estimate and bill to that. Still others the expenses are built into the fee. We treat all the same, as will be seen below, but this means in some cases there can be profit or loss on the expenses, and in some cases additional expenses can affect the fee write-offs.
