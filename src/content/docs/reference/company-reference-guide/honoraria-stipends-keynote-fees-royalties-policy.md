## Honoraria, Stipends, Keynote Fees, Royalties Policy

Occasionally in the course of work, our employees generate additional income in the form of honoraria, stipends, and the like.

In general, honoraria and stipends are granted in connection with conferences or other events that we consider primarily to be opportunities.  Typically, the firm spends money on travel expenses to attend these events and usually pays to develop the material and attend the event.  This is a substantial investment, both through wages paid and the opportunity cost of preparation and attendance.  Because of this, it is the Company’s position that a decision about which functions to attend will be rooted in the marketing value for the firm.

Further, our guideline is that fees paid in connection with speaking be sent to the firm, as the firm paid many of the costs for preparation, attendance, travel, and lodging.

Keynotes are a prime example of this.  A keynote speaking fee can be $5,000 or more.  We will view this as a mini project and expect that these fees would be billed by and received by the firm.

In most cases, book royalties fall into another category.  Currently, the Company has not commissioned any books, but, if we did commission a book, we would retain the royalty rights.  Most books are better marketing tools if they are not perceived to be marketing.  The books that employees have written to date, and those we expect someone to write in the foreseeable future, while reflective of the firm’s position, are not marketing materials for the firm.

Due to our busy schedules, most book writing is done outside standard work hours.  Apparently, many companies keep the royalties earned by employees/ authors, but we currently have not adopted this practice.  Presently, employees who author books may retain the copyright and the royalties.
