# Common Scams & Avoiding Them

More and more, various types of scams are hitting the company and it’s employees. While it is impossible to address them all, this is meant to be a guide to avoid falling into any traps. Often, these scammers attack new employees before they’ve had a chance to get their bearings.

Here are a few general types we often see:

1. Scammers appearing to be Microsoft requiring you to either change your password or click a link to keep an expired one.

   1. Our Office subscription does not expire passwords and will only require an update if reset by an admin. Always confirm with JT if you are unsure.
2. Text messages or emails appearing to be from Dave requesting a purchase for a client (sometimes gift cards). Scammers often make this sound highly urgent, that it must be done immediately or bad things will happen.

   1. Avoid this by saving Dave’s number in your phone (970) 988-9059. The SA office is (970) 490-2224. When in doubt, call or send an email to verify before making any purchase, especially a large one.
3. Requests to update bank information, or links to false payment remittances. This most often is directed at JT, but sometimes others may receive solicitations appearing to be invoices, payments, etc.

   1. Avoid this by not opening any links you are not confident are legitimate. Payroll banking information should only be communicated via Rippling and never email. When in doubt, check with Dave or JT for verification.

Generally, if you are unsure with the source of an email, best to verify via some other means (Teams chat, phone call, etc.). Sometimes scammers can mask an email to appear legitimate, but often if you click on the email it will reveal information that shows it is clearly a fake (odd name, misspellings, etc.)

If you believe your information may have been compromised, please reach out to JT and/or Jamie as soon as possible. Depending on the type of attack, there may be steps required to secure company information, change passwords, etc.

> **Tips to recognize malicious emails:**
>
> ·       The sender’s email address is not recognizable or is not associated with the sender’s name.
>
> ·       Hover over a link in the email to see if the URL is strange or unexpected.
>
> ·       Spelling and grammar errors throughout the message.
>
> ·       The content contains a threat, false sense of urgency, or requests personal information.
>
> **Security best practices:**
>
> ·       Do not open an attachment from an unknown sender.
>
> ·       Do not reply to unsolicited email or click the "Unsubscribe" link.
>
> ·       Never give away your personal information (e.g., username, password, SSN, account numbers).
>
> ·       Don’t give your email address to sites you don’t trust.
