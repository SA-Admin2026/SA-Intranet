# Ultrawrap - R2RML Exercise

**Email to Juan on 2016-01-8**

1. Needs to be super-simple, to allow students to learn everything they need to know about the tool and the approach to complete the connect to the RDB and execute SPARQL queries, all in 90 minutes. We want the students to not just watch, but to do it themselves.  To help out the slower students, we recommend they work in pairs with mixed ability.
2. I’ll be the guinea pig, as I’ve not worked with R2RML or Ultrawrap yet, and I’m not a dev guy.
3. Easy every day subject matter (ancestry, or movies or something from DBpedia or Wikidata)
4. Single table, maybe 4- 5 columns
5. Decent sized data set, maybe 10,000 rows.
6. We have an ontology ready-made that will be the basis for the connect and the SPARQL queries.
7. We use Ultrawrap to attach to the relational DB loaded with the sample data. on the Web, say from DBpedia or [ancestry.com](http://ancestry.com)
8. Compare with results of SQL queries
9. Discuss and ideally demonstrate some of the advantages and benefits of doing this.

I will be working out all the details of the exercise, in terms of what we want to do, and Scott Ogle will do the dev work when he gets back from vacation in a week or two..

I’d like to touch base with you to go over a few things.

1. You might already have some data sets that you use for this kind of thing.
2. On benefits (point 8) it would be really cool to have something compelling to say about integration with other data stores that are either also SQL or already in Triple stores using a single ontology as the glue.  I’m not sure what the architectural options are there.
3. Finally, can you share with us some of your introduction decks that explain the core technology – I can use those to best represent UltraWrap. We want the students to be inspired by the possibilities and maturity of semantic technology, and how Ultrawrap is one key tool in the toolkit..
4. Given there is just 90 min, we will have to be selective about which parts we have the students do vs. which parts are already set up for them. E.g. we might have most of the mapping done, but give them the exercise of doing the last bits.
