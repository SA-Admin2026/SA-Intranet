# How to evaluate the quality of an ontology

What should you be able to do with an ontology (in order of importance)

1. Load representative data from the domain
2. Make meaning queries against loaded data
3. Make integration easier
4. Build applications easily
5. Federate with other ontologies

Some characteristics to measure

- Coverage – does the ontology cover all or most of the concepts like to be needed in this domain (done by sampling and search)
- Distinctions-- does it provide the fine grained distinctions that are needed to support business processes and rules (for instance if underwriting says convertibles have different insurance than hardtops, we need to distinguish, convertibles from hardtops – note this does not mean they need to be separate classes)
- Understandability – can a motivated practitioner understand it is a reasonable time
- Unambiguousness – have definitions avoided unnecessary overlap.  This extends to the areas where overlap most pronounced: things v. roles, specifications v. products, books v. content, government v. country, facility v building v organization
- Reduction of clutter – have unnecessary distinctions been pruned out
- Modularity
- Time and temporality
