# How To Write "Mo-Better" Case Studies

## Why?

A customer’s Journey includes:

![](../../attachments/2395242508/2395570177.png)

When you visit a new website…

- You try to figure out what a company does
- How big are they? *Just generally*

  - Clues such professional website, recruiting, team, clients

Then…

- Check out credentials
- Have they done what they purport to do?
- Can I trust them
- Finally, can I get a sense for price (surprisingly rare)

## Guidelines

Guideline 0: Any case study is better than no case study

Guideline 1: Name names if you can

- Client names (unless sensitive) - we ask forgiveness, not permission
- Don’t say things that could cause an issue if they found out
- Also name partners if appropriate (and make sure they are listed on our partners page)

Guardrail A: Don’t claim client endorsement (unless you have a testimonial that has been cleared)

Guideline 2: Think of the case study as a corporate resume

- Focus on what we did
- Don’t take credit for something we may have only been partially responsible for

Guardrail B: Don’t claim business results

Guideline 3: Describe the domain

- Almost everyone finds the specifics interesting, and it demonstrates that we dig into the domain

Guideline 4: Tell a story

- Stories are far more engaging than a bunch of facts

Guardrail C: Don’t reveal client secrets

- Some things we might not think of are considered secret (# workers at Morgan Stanley)
- Generally safe to talk about # of apps, # of elements, etc.
- Don’t reveal initiatives they might not want revealed (working on a ChatGPT engine or something)

Guideline 5: Include pictures

- Stock photos are fine
- Don’t need to be literal
- Anything that evokes either the client, the industry or the project would help
