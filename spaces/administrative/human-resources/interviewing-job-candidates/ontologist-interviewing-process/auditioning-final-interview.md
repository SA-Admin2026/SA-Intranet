# Auditioning (Final Interview)

We call the final interview the “audition”. Auditions contain 3 parts:

1. General Questions to the candidate (roughly 45 min)

   1. Questions related to resume or previous work
   2. Behavioral Questions
2. Role Play (roughly 45 min)

   1. See extended notes below
   2. The candidate has been told to expect some modeling, and to please bring a dark pen (like a sharpie) and a few pieces of paper
3. Open Floor for candidate to question the team (15-30 min)

   1. We should always open this time with each consultant sharing what projects they are on currently and what they are doing (basically). Since we have a signed NDA, you can name your client and share pretty openly. This serves to promote the firm and get people excited about working here.

## Modeling Role Play Management

### **Background**

- The modeling exercise is designed to be applied fairly to all candidates

  - Do your best to never lead: We want to see if the candidate can elicit knowledge and move the discussion along without our assistance
  - Be aware of any bias you may come in with: Do your best to apply the same standards to all candidates, regardless of who they are or their background/experience
  - Let the candidate pursue irrelevant details, get off track, etc. This is valuable knowledge to know how a person will do in client interactions and whether they can stay focused and not flustered

### **Roles**

- We don’t want the candidate to single out one person they think is the key decision maker, so each person in the audition should adopt one of the following roles

  - Project Admin: Responsible for billing essentially
  - Payroll: Responsible for getting employees paid
  - Project Manager: Staff and project oversight
  - Sales Person: Concerned with estimating, budget, etc.
  - End User: Just an employee who needs to log time

**NOTE: Do not announce these roles directly to the candidate unsolicited. If they ask or discern this its ok to state it clearly, but don’t offer it up.**

### **Rubric for Evaluation of Model**

### **Artifact**

Focus on Level 1 first. If those are achieved and there is more time, go to Level 2.

#### **Level 1**

- Connection between time entry, person, and project
- Clients and projects
- Precision of time entry (nearest hour, or quarter-hour; not for a whole day by default)
- Billing rate vs pay rate
- Date-times and/or hours worked
- Different billing rates for different employees

#### **Level 2**

- PTO and holidays
- Different billing rates for different projects
- Different billing rates for different roles
- Different billing rates for different seniority levels
- People playing different roles on different projects
- Free text time entry log

#### **A bridge too far (things to avoid or not mention)**

- Taxes
- Currency exchange
- Location
- Different types of PTO and sick leave (e.g., vacation vs parental leave)
- Different types of contracts: e.g., T&M vs flat rate
- Salaried vs hourly employees
- Implementation of model in an application or web form

### **Elicitation**

Key Identifiers:

- Did they ask questions to identify the nature of the problem?
- Did they ask “curious questions”?
- Were they engaging with the team (good eye contact, spoke to everyone, etc.)?
- Did they ask for feedback on their model and apply it through revisions?
