## Operational Policies

 

## Employee Classifications

 Employees of the Company are classified as either exempt or nonexempt under federal and state wage and hour laws and are further classified for administrative purposes, such as the administration of certain benefits. The following classifications are used throughout this section. Full-time Employee - an employee normally scheduled to work at least 30 hours per week. Full-time employees are eligible for the employee benefits described in this Handbook and are provided with benefits required by applicable law Part-time Employee - an employee normally scheduled to work less than 30 hours per week. Part-time employees are eligible for some, but not all employee benefits described in this Handbook and are provided with benefits required by applicable law. Temporary Employee - employees who are employed for short-term assignments. Temporary employees are hired to temporarily supplement the workforce or assist in the completion of a specific project. These temporary employment assignments are of limited duration. Temporary employees are not eligible for employee benefits, except as required by applicable law and may be classified as exempt or nonexempt based on job duties and compensation. Exempt Employee - Exempt employees are employees whose job assignments meet specific tests established by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and state law and who are exempt from minimum wage and/or overtime pay requirements. Non-exempt Employee – Nonexempt employees are employees whose job positions do not meet FLSA or applicable state exemption tests, and who are not exempt from minimum wage and overtime pay requirements. Nonexempt employees are eligible to receive overtime pay for hours worked more than 40 hours in each workweek, or as otherwise required by applicable state law. 

## Personnel Files and Records

 In order to obtain their position, employees have provided personal information, such as address and telephone number. This information is contained in their personnel file. Employees should keep their personnel file up to date by informing Human Resources of any changes. Unreported changes of address, marital status, etc. can affect withholding tax and benefit coverage. Further, an "out of date" emergency contact or an inability to reach employees in a crisis could cause a severe health or safety risk or other significant problem. In accordance with CO Revised Statue 8 129, employees have the right to request once annually a copy of his or her own personnel file. A former employee may make one inspection request after termination of their employment. Records available are limited in scope per statute. The Company has 30 days from formal request to make said records available. 

## Flexible & Remote Work Policy

 We trust that our employees are providing excellent service to both internal and external stakeholders. That culture of trust is seen in the fact our employees work remotely from anywhere, allowing work from home, work while traveling, and the ability to be present where and when needed. As an employee of the company, much of your time will be spent in a remote setting. Employees are free to generally set their schedule in a way that is conducive to this arrangement, with some basic guidelines:
- Regular attendance is considered an essential function of each employee’s job and is necessary for the efficient operation of the business
- Employees are expected to attend all scheduled meetings with colleagues and/or clients unless taking paid time off. Absences or late arrivals should be communicated to those involved as often as possible. Though there are times this is not feasible, it should be the exception and not the norm. You are expected to take virtual meetings, including internal meetings, with your computer camera turned on and as distraction-free as possible (this includes backgrounds, workspaces, headphones, etc). Though remote, the goal is to attempt to capture the value face-to-face interaction provides. 

## Pay Period

 Our pay periods start at 2:01 a.m. MT on Fridays and ends at 2:00 a.m. MT on the Friday, 28 days later (four week pay period). 

## Paydays

 Paydays are every four (4) weeks. Employees should set up their banking information via Rippling. Do not ever give your banking details out via email, as this is often a phishing scheme. Pay is then directly deposited into the employee’s checking account within ten days of the end of a pay period. If you believe an improper deduction has been made to your salary, you should immediately report this information to human resources. If it is determined that an improper deduction has occurred, you will be promptly reimbursed. While every effort will be made by the Company to pay the correct amount within the correct time, you should review your paycheck when received and contact HR if you believe an error has been made. Modifications to your federal or state withholdings, as well as updating your direct deposit information must be done via Rippling. For security reasons, HR is unable to update bank account information or tax forms. 

## Time Reporting

 All employees must record all time worked daily. The Company requires all employees to utilize the current electronic timecard system to report hours worked. Time should be reported contemporaneously, using the appropriate category and with a description. We will review time spent, in addition to progress on current assignments, during our weekly morning meetings. Time entries should not span more than one day’s worth of work, though it is possible to span two calendar days if you are working late, but should not span a pay week. Be extra careful to not have an entry span across two payroll periods. If you are working late on Thursday, you should have one-time card entry that ends before 2:00 a.m. Friday (well before would be best) and another that ends after that. Pay is based on the "end date time" so the first entry would get paid in the first pay week and the later one in the following pay week. No time will be lost or double counted in any event, but if you put all the charge on a line that ends after midnight the whole amount will be paid in the subsequent time period. Pay weeks end at 2:00 a.m. (Mountain Time) on Friday. It is your responsibility to ensure your total hours according to the time keeping system match your expectations. Discrepancies or questions should be brought to HR as soon as possible to avoid any issues at payroll time. 

## Off-The-Clock Work

 It is a clear violation of labor laws for employees to engage in “off the clock” work, where you are performing work for the benefit of the company and not reporting it for compensation. Ultimately, if this practice is discovered the employer is held liable (so even if you think you are doing the firm a favor to save a few bucks, you are actually opening it to a very large lawsuit liability). Hourly employees are expressly prohibited from off-the-clock work. Employees found to be misreporting time (either under or over actual worked hours) are subject to discipline up to and including termination. 

## Overtime

 This policy outlines our company's standards for overtime pay, in compliance with the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and applicable state laws where we operate. Under Section 13(a)(1) and Section 13(a)(17), skilled computer workers are generally exempt from overtime pay, as long as they are paid greater than the minimum required hourly rate. There are also exceptions for administrative employees who serve a primary role in directing the administrative functions of the business. The handling of overtime pay is important for two reasons. First, Semantic Arts must ensure we compensate employees fairly and in accordance with relevant laws. Second, Semantic Arts must ensure that the additional costs of overtime (whether exempt overtime or not) align with business priorities and serve a bona fide business purpose. 

## Overtime Pay Requirements

 Based on the type of employment and job duties for a given role, you are designated as “exempt” or “non-exempt” under the FLSA standards. These are outlined below. Employee Type Overtime Pay Requirements Executive Stao (CEO, Pres, COO) Exempt from overtime pay Salaried Administrative Stao Exempt from overtime pay Hourly Administrative Stao Non-exempt from overtime pay Internal Semantic Developers Generally exempt from overtime pay, with exceptions Consultants Generally exempt from overtime pay, with exceptions Exceptions are state-specific. For instance: - In Washington, computer workers must be paid a salary to be exempt from overtime requirements. - The Colorado COMPS order creates a higher threshold for CO employees. - In California, employees paid less than $56.97 are non-exempt from overtime pay. Based on the above, non-exempt employees are entitled to overtime pay at 1.5x their regular pay rate for all time worked over 40 hours a week. The 40 hours are calculated on worked time, meaning that PTO does not count into the calculation. In California, any hours worked over 8 in a single workday are also counted as overtime and must be paid at the higher rate. Project managers and supervisors are expected to monitor spending and manage teams appropriately. We also expect employees to make the most of their time, ensuring that all time recorded is being done to benefit and support the firm. All time is subject to review by management, and management reserves the right to limit how employees utilize their time. Employees living in states with more restrictive regulations, such as those cited above, will be dealt with on a case-by-case basis, and may be subject to additional restrictions on overtime hours. 

## Travel Time for Non-Exempt Employees

 Overnight, Out-of-Town Trips Non-exempt employees will be compensated for time spent traveling (except for meal periods) during their normal working hours, on days they are scheduled to work and on unscheduled work days (such as weekends). Non-exempt employees also will be paid for any time spent performing job duties during otherwise non-compensable travel time; however, such work should be limited absent advance management authorization. Out-of-Town Trips for One Day Non-exempt employees who travel out of town for a one-day assignment will be paid for all travel time, except for, among other things: time spent traveling between the employee's home and the local railroad, bus or plane terminal; and meal periods. Local Travel Non-exempt employees will be compensated for time spent traveling from one job site to another job site during a workday. The trip home, however, is non-compensable when the employee goes directly home from the final job site, unless it is much longer than the regular commute home from the regular worksite. In such case, the portion of the trip home in excess of the regular commute is compensable. Commuting Time Under the Portal to Portal Act, travel from home to work and from work to home is generally non-compensable. However, if a non-exempt employee regularly reports to a worksite near their home, but is required to report to a worksite farther away than the regular worksite, the additional time spent traveling is compensable. If compensable travel time results in more than 40 hours worked by a non-exempt employee, the employee will be compensated at an overtime rate of one and one-half (1.5) times the regular rate. 

## Safe Harbor Policy for Salaried Exempt Employees

 It is Semantic Arts’ policy and practice to accurately compensate employees and to do so in compliance with all applicable state and federal laws. To ensure proper payment and that no improper deductions are made, employees must review pay stubs promptly to identify and report all errors. Those classified as exempt salaried employees will receive a salary which is intended to compensate them for all hours they may work for Semantic Arts. This salary will be established at the time of hire or classification as an exempt employee. While it may be subject to review and modification from time to time, such as during salary review times, the salary will be a predetermined amount that will not be subject to deductions for variations in the quantity or quality of the work performed. Under federal and state law, salary is subject to certain deductions. For example, unless state law requires otherwise, salary can be reduced for the following reasons:
- full-day absences for personal reasons;
- full-day absences for sickness or disability if the deduction is made in accordance with a bona fide plan, policy or practice of providing wage replacement benefits for such absences (deductions also may be made for the exempt employee's full-day absences due to sickness or disability before the employee has qualified for the plan, policy or practice or after the employee has exhausted the leave allowance under the plan);
- full-day disciplinary suspensions for infractions of our written policies and procedures;
- Family and Medical Leave Act absences (either full- or partial-day absences);
- to offset amounts received as payment from the court for jury and witness fees or from the military as military pay;
- the first or last week of employment in the event the employee works less than a full week; and
- any full work week in which the employee does not perform any work. Salary may also be reduced for certain types of deductions such as a portion of health, dental or life insurance premiums; state, federal or local taxes; social security; or voluntary contributions to a 401(k) or pension plan. In any work week in which the employee performed any work, salary will not be reduced for any of the following reasons:
- partial day absences for personal reasons, sickness or disability;
- an absence because the Company has decided to close a facility on a scheduled work day;
- absences for jury duty, attendance as a witness, or military leave in any week in which the employee performed any work (subject to any offsets as set forth above); and
- any other deductions prohibited by state or federal law. However, unless state law provides otherwise, deductions may be made to accrued leave for full- or partial-day absences for personal reasons, sickness or disability. If employees believe they have been subject to any improper deductions, they should immediately report the matter to a supervisor. If the supervisor is unavailable or if the employee believes it would be inappropriate to contact that person (or if the employee has not received a prompt and fully acceptable reply), they should immediately contact Human Resources or any other supervisor in Semantic Arts with whom the employee feels comfortable.
