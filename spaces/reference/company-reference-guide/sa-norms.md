# SA Norms

The goal of this page is to document some things that aren’t quite policies, but norms around how we work at Semantic Arts. Suggestions to include here should be routed to [office@semanticarts.com](mailto:office@semanticarts.com).

## Conflict Resolution

Generally speaking, we want to normalize having direct, one-on-one conversations, especially when there is an issue or complaint with someone in leadership.

If you have an issue with any staff member, the goal is to take it straight to that person. This includes the CEO, president, COO, and members of the governance committee. If you still feel the issue is not resolved, then take it to the appropriate person in leadership. There will be times a matter is sensitive and may not be appropriate to bring directly, but that should be the exception and not the norm.

When issues and complaints are not handled directly, conversation can sometimes go sideways and lead to deterioration of company culture. We have a good culture here and it is important we do what we can to preserve it.

This relates to our ‘One-Voice” company value. Rather than side conversations about an issue, which could go in multiple directions and may be just speculation, handle the issue more directly and with the source to avoid multiple and possibly untrue 'stories’ going around.

## Staff Planning

The art of assigning consultants to projects is more complex than many realize. So much so, it’s done by committee in a weekly meeting. **Ultimately, the President carries final authority for assigning staff.** Some of the factors that contribute to these decisions include:

- Current availability and likelihood of current project renewal.

o   It is preferable to keep project teams stable over multiple task orders, though not guaranteed as other circumstances may factor in.

- Time on the bench: Those that have been on the bench longer will generally get preference in upcoming projects, though special circumstances may warrant exceptions.

- Background/Expertise: If a particular project requires a certain skill or technology stack, it may make sense to assign someone with that skillset.

- Domain: Domain experience matters, but not as much. It is good for our team to become well-rounded and to avoid relying on domain experts so that a project’s success doesn’t hinge on availability of one or two people.

- Experience: Projects need a mix of senior, mid, and entry level resources.

- Preference: Known preferences by consultants are factored in, but again not as greatly. We will do what we can to assign people based on feedback, but ultimately the needs of the business will come first.

This is not formulaic, and it fluctuates often. We now have a practice of not including individual consultant names in our proposals to avoid locking up staff if a project is delayed. We will do our best to communicate when we *think* we have a consultant positioned on an upcoming project, but again this is fluid and can change frequently.

Due to the way consultant pay is structured, Semantic Arts and the employees are both incentivized to spend as much time on billable work as possible, so consultants can be confident this is the ultimate goal of the sales team.

Similar to our philosophy on role promotions, self-promotion is likely to work against you in staff planning. Making a preference known is ok, repeatedly doing so is not. Working to get yourself added to a project or to manipulate the staffing of a project is not only frowned upon, it may result in disciplinary action.

## Ethical Exemptions

Different people have different ethical standards. As such, we do not take a position on what those standards should be for the company as a whole. However, individuals may exercise their own ethical principles by refusing work with particular clients or on particular projects on ethical grounds. Specifically:

- Consultants have the right to refuse placement on any project based on ethical considerations. They will retain their place in the bench line-up.
- Consultants should voice their concerns with the president and/or HR as soon as they learn they are being considered for the project or earlier, if they wish.
- If requested, ethical exemptions will be kept confidential and shared only between the president and HR.
- Consultants may not *refuse* a client placement for any other reason. You may express *preferences* *and concerns* about present or future assignments (based on, for example, personal circumstances or professional interests) to the president and/or HR. These will be taken seriously, but, due to the constraints described above under [Staff Planning](https://semarts.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AD/pages/3029303298/SA+Norms#Staff-Planning), we cannot promise a particular outcome.
